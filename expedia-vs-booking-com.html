Why the two prices are so often identical

Expedia and Booking.com are both large online travel agencies, and both sign rate-parity agreements with the hotels they list. In practice that pushes the public nightly rate toward a match across channels, so the number you see on Expedia is often close to the one on Booking.com. Across our 14 snapshots of September 25 to October 1, 2026, 3,715 hotels were listed on both. Where both prices were on the same tax footing (3,056 hotels), their lowest prices were within $2 a night of each other at 59% of them; Expedia was more than 2% cheaper at 945 (31%) and Booking.com at 268 (9%).

So, on the same footing, neither site was always cheaper for the same room, but when their prices differed, Expedia was the cheaper one about 3.5 times as often as Booking.com (945 hotels to 268). When Expedia was the cheaper one, it was typically 9% below Booking.com. The differences that actually move money live in three places most people never check: the all-in total after taxes and mandatory fees, the loyalty programs, and the member or mobile-only rates each site can offer once you sign in.

Before tax or after

At the 2,652 hotels listing both where taxes and fees added 10% or more to the going rate (the price most sites agreed on), Expedia's price sat nearer the before-tax going rate at 46 of them and Booking.com's at 212. Where one of the two prices looked before tax and the other after (242 hotels), the before-tax one looked lower at every one. Part or all of such a gap can be tax that is added at checkout, so those hotels are left out of the counts above, and so are the 417 hotels where taxes added between 2% and 10%, where neither price's basis could be read. Leaving out the hotels whose going rates showed under 2% tax, 2,410 hotels remain, where taxes added 10% or more and each price's basis could be read: Expedia was more than 2% cheaper at 760 of them and Booking.com at 165.

How we measured: in each snapshot we recorded every booking site's price that Google Hotels showed a US searcher, in US dollars, and for each hotel took each site's lowest price, all-in with taxes and fees where the site reported them, otherwise its listed rate. Our data keeps one price per listing without saying whether it includes tax, so we infer it: where the hotel's after-tax going rate was 10% or more above its before-tax going rate, a price nearer the after-tax one counts as after tax and a price nearer the before-tax one as before tax; where the after-tax going rate was under 2% above the before-tax one (often because no site reported taxes separately), any two prices count as the same footing; hotels in between are left out. Within $2 means the two differed by $2 a night or less; more than 2% cheaper means one was below 98% of the other. The two can overlap on cheaper rooms (a $1.50 gap on a $60 room is both), which happened at 29 of these 3,056 hotels. A typical gap is the median, rounded down. What each site is actually better at

Neither is a blanket winner. Reach for whichever fits the trip in front of you.

Expedia tends to win when…

  • • You're booking a flight plus hotel together: package rates can undercut booking each part alone.
  • • You use One Key and want OneKeyCash that also works on Hotels.com and Vrbo.
  • • A member or mobile-app rate knocks a chunk off once you're signed in.
  • • You're already inside the Expedia ecosystem and want one place for flights, cars, and stays.

Booking.com tends to win when…

  • • Its Genius discount or a secret rate beats the rest on your dates.
  • • You want the widest property list, including small independents and lots of Europe.
  • • You value broad free-cancellation options and one dashboard for several stays.
  • • The all-in total, after fees, simply comes out lower here for your room.

Notice that almost none of these are the headline rate; they're loyalty value, packaging, and fees. That's the honest catch: a $4-cheaper rate on one site can cost you more once a resort fee, a loyalty credit, or a stricter cancellation policy is in the math. Weigh the all-in price and the terms together.

The differences that actually move money

  • Loyalty: One Key vs Genius. Expedia's One Key earns OneKeyCash after the stay across Expedia, Hotels.com, and Vrbo. Booking.com's Genius discounts you at checkout, typically 10 to 20% plus perks at participating hotels. One rewards later, one lowers the price now: compare the actual credit or discount against each site's all-in price.
  • The all-in total. Taxes and mandatory resort fees can appear at different steps of checkout on each site. A lower headline rate can quietly carry a higher final total, so always read the price after fees on both.
  • Member and mobile-only rates. Signing in (or booking on the app) sometimes unlocks a lower price on either site than the one you see logged out. It's worth a quick check before you decide.
  • Packaging. If you also need a flight or car, Expedia's bundles can beat the sum of the parts. If you only need the room, that advantage disappears.

And when a third site shows a much lower price than either Expedia or Booking.com, slow down: the lowest-priced listing is often an unverifiable wholesaler rate, and it may not be the same room or terms. That's exactly the kind of gap worth confirming before you switch.

How to actually decide, in two minutes

  1. Open the same room and dates on both. Read the price after taxes and fees on Expedia and on Booking.com; the headline rate alone will usually tie.
  2. Sign in and re-check. Member and mobile rates, plus a Genius discount or a One Key credit, can break the tie once you're logged in.
  3. Add the hotel's own site as a third quote. Because of parity it's often close, but loyalty points and perks usually only earn when you book direct, which can tip the value.
  4. If you're not in a hurry, watch it. Both sites move with demand. Book a refundable rate and rebook if the price drops.

This is the same discipline behind every honest way to get a cheaper hotel rate: compare like-for-like on the all-in total, and don't assume one site beats the other. It also explains why prices differ across sites in the first place.

Expedia vs Booking.com: common questions

Is Expedia or Booking.com cheaper for hotels?+

For the same dates, neither is always cheaper, but Expedia was cheaper more often: across our 14 price snapshots of September 25 to October 1, 2026, Google Hotels listed both at 3,715 hotels, and where both prices were on the same tax footing (3,056 hotels), Expedia's lowest price was more than 2% below Booking.com's at 945 of them (31%) and Booking.com's below Expedia's at 268 (9%). At the 242 hotels where one price looked before tax and the other after, the before-tax one looked lower at every one, so those are not counted. Rate parity pushes the headline nightly rate toward a match, so on the same footing the two landed within $2 a night at 59% of hotels. The difference shows up in the all-in total after fees, in the loyalty programs, and in member or mobile rates. Compare the same room's all-in total on both and take the lower confirmed one.

Why is the same hotel the same price on both?+

Hotels sign rate-parity clauses with large agencies that discourage undercutting, which pulls the public rate together but not all the way: within $2 a night at 59% of the 3,056 hotels where both prices were on the same tax footing (3,715 hotels listed both across our 14 price snapshots of September 25 to October 1, 2026). The real levers sit elsewhere: One Key versus Genius, fees surfaced at different points of checkout, and members-only or app-only rates. That's why comparing the all-in total on both beats assuming one site is cheaper.

One Key or Genius: which loyalty program is better?+

One Key earns OneKeyCash after your stay across Expedia, Hotels.com, and Vrbo; Genius discounts you at checkout, typically 10 to 20% plus perks at participating hotels. One rewards you later, the other takes money off the price now. Which wins depends on how often you book and whether the specific property participates, so compare the actual credit or discount against each site's all-in price.

Is it safe to book through Expedia or Booking.com?+

Both are large, established sites and are generally safe. The usual complaints are about service and flexibility, not fraud: changes can be slower through a third party than booking direct, and a low headline rate can hide a resort fee that only appears at checkout. Read the cancellation terms and the all-in total before you commit.
