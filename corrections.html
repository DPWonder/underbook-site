Corrections

Every time we found that something we had published was wrong, or said more than we could back, we changed it. This is the list, newest first: what we said, what was wrong, what we changed and when. It has 67 entries, from July 2026.

Found something wrong? Email hi@underbook.co. How the product works, and what we measured about its accuracy, is on How Underbook works.

What this log covers

  • Figures and claims we published: on this site and in our price study, in the extension's banner, in our store listings, in the emails we send to people who set a price watch, and in press emails we sent.
  • Not listed: mistakes we caught before anything went out, and figures we refreshed when new price snapshots arrived, unless the earlier figure was wrong.
  • Times are UTC. "About" means our records give only the hour. Extension changes reach each browser when it updates, which can take a few days after the store publishes a version.

Extension and website

"All-in" said every fee was included

What we said
The banner's fine print, our price-drop emails and several pages said "All-in = taxes & fees included", and /resort-fees-explained said Underbook's all-in price had "resort fees and taxes included".
What was wrong
Our prices come from what each booking site reports to Google Hotels, and that can leave out a fee the hotel charges on top. On October 19, 2026 one Booking.com page in our sample (Sonesta Denver Downtown) listed a $25 a night destination fee only in its fine print, outside the price we showed.
What we changed
Everywhere we say all-in, we now say "taxes and fees as each site reports them; a resort or destination fee may be extra". On a Booking.com page that shows a fee, the banner names it and says a lower site's price may leave it out. Extension 0.5.56 (Firefox and Edge from October 20; Chrome when its review clears), emails from October 20, website October 20 and 21, and the web price checker's results, help line and share message on October 22 (until then they said "all in" without the resort-fee caveat).

Comparison pagesabout 23:15 UTC

Ten site-versus-site pages compared before-tax prices with all-in ones

What we said
KAYAK vs Expedia said KAYAK listed the lower price about twice as often (692 to 357 of 1,537 hotels). Agoda vs Expedia said Agoda about 1.6 times as often, and Hotels.com vs Agoda said Agoda a little more often.
What was wrong
Google Hotels shows some sites' prices before tax and others with tax included. These ten pages compared each site's lowest price as listed, so a before-tax price next to an all-in one looked lower when it was not. Where tax added 10% or more, KAYAK's price sat nearer the before-tax rate at 506 of 1,311 hotels; Expedia's at 23.
What we changed
All ten pages now count only hotels where both prices were on the same tax footing, and each page says what it said before. Three conclusions reversed: Expedia was lower about 2.6 times as often as KAYAK (313 to 120 of 847 hotels), Expedia a little more often than Agoda (363 to 269 of 1,770), and Hotels.com a little more often than Agoda (403 to 320 of 1,959). Priceline vs Booking.com went from neither clearly lower to Booking.com a little more often (388 to 327 of 3,629). On the other six pages the same site stays lower, by different margins.

Some study prices were listed for travelers in other countries

What we said
The price study's two October 1 runs (25 US cities and 25 international cities) count 578 hotels with a listing 5% or more below the going rate, and the study page and the hotel pages give each one's lowest-priced site and price as prices for a traveler in the US.

Stricter "same room name" rules, after a test we failed

What we said
