This week in hotel prices

What we measured on October 10, 2026: 381 hotels in 23 cities, each priced on every booking site Google Hotels listed for the same 2-night stay, November 14 to November 16, 2026 (35 days out), 2 adults, as a traveler in the US, in US dollars (one run). These are one day's prices; prices change daily.

  1. 1. 292 of 381 hotels had a listing 5% or more below the going rate

    In the October 10, 2026 snapshot, 292 of the 381 hotels with a price (76.6%) had at least one booking site listing the stay 5% or more below the going rate, the most common price among listings that named a room or offered free cancellation. Where one did, the median gap was 16.7%, about $22 a night before taxes and fees. In the 16 earlier snapshots (each run counted on its own) the share ranged from 61.2% to 80.8%; this snapshot was inside that range.

  2. 2. Super.com had the lowest price most often

    Among the 292 hotels with a listing 5% or more below the going rate, Super.com had the lowest price at 52 (17.8%), then Traveluro at 41 and the hotel's own site at 29. At 250 of the 292 (85.6%), the lowest-priced listing named no room, so its price alone does not say which room it buys.

  3. 3. At 34% of hotels, another site listed free cancellation for the same price or less

    At 128 of 381 hotels, the lowest rate on at least one of Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com or Agoda had no free cancellation while another site listed free cancellation for the same price or less (within 2%). The label is the one Google Hotels showed; the deadline and terms are on each site. More: is free cancellation worth it?

  4. 4. Hotels priced again: the going rate was a median 0.8% higher

    We priced again the 230 hotels of 13 cities whose newest snapshot was the oldest; 221 had a going rate both times. For a stay 12 to 15 days later, at the same 35 days out, their going rate was a median 0.8% higher: more than 2% up at 105 and more than 2% down at 96. Of the 167 that had a listing 5% or more below the going rate before, 138 still had one, and at 48 of those the lowest-priced site was the same. Two different stays: this is how the same hotels' prices for the same lead time moved, not the same stay priced twice.

  5. 5. 245 of 381 answers were priced for a traveler in the US

    Every search asked Google Hotels for prices for a traveler in the US, in US dollars. By the booking sites' links, 245 of the 381 answers with a price were priced for a visitor in the US, 129 for a visitor in another country and 7 named no country. A site that prices by the visitor's country can show a US traveler a different price: the caveat, with what we found.

Method and data

The going rate is the rate the largest number of listings agree on, with ties broken low; a cheaper listing is one 5% or more below it. A new city's hotels are the first 20 hotel-type results Google Hotels returns for "hotels in city"; a city priced again keeps the hotels of its previous snapshot, each priced by its Google property token. Prices are as Google Hotels listed each site that day; sites it did not list are not in the data. Full method, every earlier snapshot and the caveats: the hotel price dispersion study.
