Free extension for Chrome, Edge and Firefox

Is there a browser extension that compares hotel prices?
Yes, several free ones.

Yes. Several free browser extensions, among them Hotel Ninja, Directo, RatePunk and Underbook, read the hotel and dates on the booking page you are viewing and show what other booking sites charge for that stay, with no new search. Underbook runs on Chrome, Edge and Firefox, checks every booking site Google Hotels lists for your dates, all-in where the sites report taxes and fees, and says whether a lower price names the same room.

Something like Honey, but for hotel prices? What it does on a hotel page

Open a hotel on a booking site as you normally would. The extension reads the property and the dates already on screen, then asks what the other sites are charging for that same stay. A single banner appears with the answer. There is no separate tab, no second search to retype, and no form to fill in.

Every site Google Hotels lists

Mainstream booking sites alongside the discount resellers that usually hold the lowest listed number.

All-in, not headline

Compared with taxes and fees included where the source reports them, because the nightly rate on the page is often not what you pay at checkout.

Flagged, not hidden

If the cheaper listing does not name the room it is selling, the banner says so instead of presenting it as a like-for-like win.

Does the price it shows hold when you click through?

On October 1, 2026 we asked Underbook's price service about 19 stays, the way the extension does, and opened the listing it would show through its own link, 11 to 37 seconds later. Two stays came back with no prices, so 17 pages were opened. 11 could be read: at 9 the page showed our price within 1.5%, on the same tax basis; at 2, both Agoda, it showed a higher price before tax (+5.6% and +11.7%), on offers Google had priced for visitors in another country. None of the listings was gone. 6 pages could not be read (bot checks on Super.com and Expedia, and two hotels' own booking pages).

In two larger audits on October 2 and 3, 2026, 40 of the 44 readable booking-site pages showed the price within 2%: is Google Hotels accurate?

So the price shown was usually the price on the page. The misses we saw were prices set for another country's visitors, members-only prices, one lower price on the page and one price shown before tax. A look at the total on the booking page before you pay settles it.

Why the lowest number is so often not what it seems

On a typical comparison, the mainstream booking sites cluster tightly at one price and publish exactly which room they are selling. The sites well below them are usually reselling wholesale inventory, and they frequently publish no room name and no cancellation terms at all.

So a large apparent discount is often not the same product. It can be a smaller room, a non-refundable rate, or a booking the hotel has no record of until you arrive. That is not a reason to never use those sites. It is a reason to know which kind of number you are looking at before you click.

This is the entire reason the banner distinguishes between a cheaper listing whose room name matches and one it cannot match. Collapsing those two into one cheerful "you saved" message would be the easy thing to build, and it would be dishonest.

How this differs from the comparison sites

Metasearch sites like trivago, Kayak and Google Hotels do compare prices, and they do it well. The difference is where the comparison happens. They require you to leave what you are doing and search again in their interface, which is also an interface with commercial ranking in it.

An extension inverts that. The comparison comes to the page you already chose, for the dates you already picked, and then gets out of the way. There is no ranked list to scroll and no sponsored row at the top.

The tradeoff is honest to state: a metasearch site can show you hotels you have not thought of, and an extension cannot. It only answers one question, which is whether the hotel in front of you is priced well.

If you already booked

Comparison only helps before you pay. After that, the useful move is watching. If you booked a refundable rate, a later price drop is money you can still recover by rebooking at the lower rate and cancelling the old reservation.

Underbook can watch a stay and email you once if the going rate for your dates is at least 5% lower on two daily checks in a row. That part needs an email address and nothing else, and it works without installing the extension at all.

Common questions

Is there a browser extension that compares hotel prices?

Yes. Free extensions such as Hotel Ninja, Directo, RatePunk and Underbook show what other booking sites charge for the hotel and dates on the page you are viewing. Underbook installs on Chrome, Edge and Firefox, checks every booking site Google Hotels lists, all-in where the sites report taxes and fees, and says whether a lower price names the same room; on a phone, its web price checker does the same check with no install.

Is there a Honey for hotels?

Honey itself does not compare hotel prices, going by its own pages read October 6, 2026. PayPal Honey looks for coupon codes as you shop: its home page names booking travel among the places it looks, and its help center says hotel and other travel reservations can earn PayPal Rewards points, which clear only once the stay is completed. Its price-drop list, Droplist, works on select stores such as Amazon, Macy's, Target and Walmart. None of those pages, nor its Chrome Web Store listing, says it compares a hotel's price across booking sites. The extensions in the table above do that, Underbook among them: on the hotel page you are viewing, it shows the price each booking site lists on Google Hotels for your dates, all-in where the sites report taxes and fees, and says whether a lower listing names the same room. It does not look for coupon codes.

How often does it find a lower hotel price?

In our hotel price study (17 snapshots from September 22 to October 10, 2026, 7,044 hotel prices in 368 cities), priced with the same rules the extension runs, some site listed the stay 5% or more below the going rate at 5,114 hotels (73%), typically 16.7% below. At 4,294 of those 5,114 the lowest listing named no room, and at 1,930 hotels (27%) no site was 5% or more below the going rate. In the 16 snapshots that compared room names, a lower listing whose room name matched turned up at 571 of 6,620 hotels (8.6%). A lower number usually exists; whether it buys your room is usually unknown.

Is there a hotel price comparison extension for Firefox or Edge?

Yes. Underbook is on Firefox Add-ons, for Firefox on a computer and Firefox for Android 142 or newer, and on Microsoft Edge Add-ons, as well as the Chrome Web Store; it is free, with no account. In the table above, Directo and RatePunk also have Firefox Add-ons listings, and Hotel Ninja's site links the Chrome Web Store only, as each said when we read them on October 5, 2026. Stayly's Firefox Add-ons listing, read October 10, 2026, is marked for Firefox and Firefox for Android.

Can I compare hotel prices across booking sites on my phone?

Yes, without comparing by hand. Underbook's web price checker at underbook.co/hotel-price-checker runs the same check in any phone browser with no install: type the hotel and your dates and it lists the price each booking site shows on Google Hotels, all-in where the sites report taxes and fees, and says whether a lower listing names the same room. On an iPhone or iPad, Underbook for Safari is free on the App Store since November 10, 2026 (iOS 16 or later) and runs the check on the hotel page you are viewing in Safari. On Android, the Underbook extension also runs in Firefox for Android 142 or newer. Most extensions in the table above name no phone version.

Does the extension work on every booking site?

It checks by itself when you open a hotel page on Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda or a hotel's own site with your dates. On any other hotel page, click its toolbar button to run the check. On booking sites' search results it shows a one-time tip and checks nothing, and it does not price short-term rental listings, which are not hotel inventory and cannot be priced against hotel sources.

What data does it collect?

The check does not run only in your browser: each check sends Underbook's price service the hotel name, its town when the page shows it, your dates, the number of adults, the booking site's market and currency, and the extension's version, and never your identity, your browsing history or payment details. On a page that is not a hotel page it sends nothing about the page, only an anonymous once-a-day usage ping with no address, title or page content. There is no account, and comparing requires no email address. If you choose to watch a price, that email is used only for emails about that stay (at most one of them a drop alert) and nothing else. An older Chrome Web Store description said the check ran in your own browser; that was wrong, and our corrections log records the fix, live September 29, 2026. Full policy: privacy.

Do you get paid when I book?

Some booking links can carry an affiliate parameter, which is the normal arrangement in this category. It does not change which price is shown lowest, because the listings are sorted by the price each site reports. The result you see would be identical either way.

Will it slow down the page?

The comparison runs after the page loads and renders as a single banner. It does not modify the booking site's own content or interfere with the checkout you are using.

Is there a browser extension that tracks hotel prices over time?

Comparing and tracking are two different jobs and it is worth being clear about which you need. Comparing answers "is this price good right now" and happens in one shot on the page. Tracking answers "did it get cheaper since I booked" and has to keep watching for days or weeks. Underbook does both, but the tracking runs on our side and emails you, rather than requiring the browser to stay open. That means a price drop still reaches you on a day you never opened your browser.

Is it really free?

Yes. There is no paid tier, no trial and no account. If that changes, it will be said plainly here rather than discovered at a paywall.
