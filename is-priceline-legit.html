The verdict, in plain terms

Legit, and sometimes cheaper than the hotel's own site, though usually not; its Express Deals trade away flexibility and certainty about which hotel you're getting.

Where Priceline is genuinely good

  • ✅ Real, major platform backed by Booking Holdings
  • ✅ Express Deals can beat the public rate meaningfully
  • ✅ Standard named-hotel bookings work like any other site
  • ✅ Good for firm plans where star rating and area are enough

Where you should be careful

  • ⚠️ Express Deals hide the hotel name until after you pay
  • ⚠️ Those rates are non-refundable with no changes
  • ⚠️ Resort fees and taxes can still be added on top
  • ⚠️ Problems go through Priceline, not the hotel front desk

Why is it sometimes cheaper than the hotel's own site?

This is the question that makes people suspicious, and the answer is mostly boring, not shady. Priceline's biggest discounts come from Express Deals: hotels quietly release rooms at a lower rate on the condition that their name stays hidden until after you book, so they can fill empty rooms without publicly undercutting their own published price. You see the neighborhood, the star rating, the guest score, and the main amenities, but not the property name until the charge goes through. We've written a full, separate breakdown of whether Priceline Express Deals are worth it, including how travelers narrow down the hidden hotel before paying.

How often a named rate is cheaper, from our September 25 snapshot (stays 35 days out, US market, USD): at the 242 hotels where Google Hotels listed both Priceline and the hotel's own site, Priceline was more than 1% cheaper at 41, within 1% at 122, and more than 1% higher at 79. Express Deals are not listed there, so we have not measured those. When an Express Deal is cheaper, what you trade for the lower price is certainty and flexibility: Express Deals are prepaid and non-refundable, you can't change or cancel, and you only find out exactly which hotel you booked after you pay. Resort fees and local taxes can also be added at the property or at checkout, so the headline can look lower than the all-in total. That is a fine trade when your dates are locked and the area and star rating are enough to commit, and a worse one when they aren't.

Before you book a prepaid Priceline rate, check three things

  • 1. The all-in total. Compare the price after taxes and fees, including any resort fee, not the headline nightly rate.
  • 2. What you actually get. On an Express Deal, weigh the discount against not knowing the exact hotel until you've paid.
  • 3. The cancellation terms. A $40 difference isn't worth it if your plans might change and the rate is non-refundable.

Priceline: common questions

Is Priceline a scam?+

No. It's a real, established booking platform owned by Booking Holdings, and the hotel bookings are genuine. The frustration people report is almost always about the terms, non-refundable Express Deals, not knowing the exact hotel until after paying, and resort fees added at the property, rather than the booking not existing. Read what an Express Deal does and doesn't promise, and confirm the all-in total, before you commit and you avoid the most common complaints.

Will I know which hotel I'm booking?+

On a standard named booking, yes. On an Express Deal, no, not until after you pay. You see the neighborhood, star rating, guest review score, and key amenities, but the exact property name stays hidden until the booking is confirmed. That's the mechanism that unlocks the lower price. If knowing the specific hotel matters to you, book a named rate instead and compare its all-in total the normal way.

Can I cancel a Priceline booking?+

It depends on the rate. Some named bookings are refundable within a window; Express Deals are non-refundable with no changes. Cancellations and problems go through Priceline rather than the hotel, which is slower than dealing with the property directly. Always check the specific rate's terms before paying, this is the single most important thing to verify on any prepaid rate.
