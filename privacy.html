Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 18, 2026

Underbook helps you find a lower hotel rate. We collect as little as possible: the extension does something on its own only on a hotel page and, right after you book on Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com or Agoda, on that site's booking confirmation page, where it offers to watch the booking and sends nothing about the booking unless you ask it to (from version 0.5.66 it counts, with no identifier, that the offer was shown and on which of those four sites). From version 0.5.67, for 24 hours after you press a banner's button to a booking site, it also counts, with no identifier, whether that site's checkout or booking confirmation page was reached. If you turn on its price-drop mark (it is off unless you do), it may also look at the prices of the stays you asked for again, once a day for up to 14 days, to mark its icon if the going rate falls. It sends the hotel name (and the town the page lists it in), your dates and your party size to our price service, and we never see your card or any other payment details. The only personal data we store is the email you give us if you choose to set a price-drop alert, and, if you tell us you already booked the stay, the price you paid and your free-cancellation date.

The store privacy labels, in plain words

Each store asks us to tick the kinds of data Underbook handles. These are the boxes we tick, and the data behind each one:

  • Personally identifiable information (Chrome Web Store, Microsoft Edge Add-ons), personally identifying information (Firefox Add-ons) and email address (App Store): your email address, only if you set a price-drop alert or a booked watch.
  • Financial and payment information (Chrome Web Store, Microsoft Edge Add-ons, Firefox Add-ons) and purchase history (App Store): for a booked watch only, the price you paid per night and its currency, your free-cancellation date and the name of the site you booked on. Never a card number, bank details or any other payment detail.
  • Website content (Chrome Web Store, Microsoft Edge Add-ons, Firefox Add-ons): the hotel name, its town, your dates and the number of adults, read from the hotel page you are on and sent to our price service for each check.
  • Browsing activity (Firefox Add-ons) and browsing history (App Store, not linked to you): the same hotel, town, dates and party for each check, and which of six booking sites a banner was on (or "other"). Never a list of the pages you visit.
  • Other user content (App Store): the stay you ask us to watch (hotel, town, dates, adults, market and currency) and one word for where you set the watch, kept with your email.
  • User activity (Chrome Web Store, Microsoft Edge Add-ons), technical and interaction data (Firefox Add-ons) and product interaction (App Store, not linked to you): the anonymous usage events described below, such as a banner was shown or a button was pressed, with no identifier.

We never see or keep: card numbers or any other payment details, booking or confirmation numbers, your name, phone number or home address, passwords, or the addresses of the pages you visit. Your IP address is not stored (the web price checker counts its daily limit under a one-way code made from it, deleted after about 26 hours). "No tracking" on the App Store means what Apple means by it: nothing we collect is combined with other companies' data for advertising or shared with data brokers.

When the extension is active

The extension can run on the websites you visit, but on its own it shows something on the page only in three places: a hotel page (a page where it can identify a specific hotel, from the page's title or metadata, and your check-in and check-out dates), a small tip on a booking site it reads, and a booking confirmation page (described below). On a booking site it reads (Booking.com, Expedia and its sister sites, Hotels.com, Agoda, Trip.com, Priceline, KAYAK, and the hotel chains' own sites it supports), the Underbook icon in your toolbar shows "ON", and on a hotel page it shows a mark while a check runs and when it is done. In Safari on iPhone and iPad that mark is the badge next to Underbook in Safari's page menu. That happens inside your browser: the extension tells its own icon what to show and sends nothing to us about it. On such a site's home page or hotel search results, it may show a small tip saying it works there: the first time a new install is on a booking site's hotel search results for real dates, this is a one-time tip saying to open a hotel from the list. It shows at most one tip a day, at most once every 14 days on each site, never on a site where you compared a hotel in the last 14 days, and never again once you press "Don't show tips again". It counts that a tip was shown, and that tips were turned off, as anonymous daily totals (the event name only: no URL, site, destination or search). Third, on the booking confirmation page that Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com or Agoda shows right after you book, it may show one card offering to watch that booking (see "Watching a booking from its confirmation page" below). It shows that card at most once for each booking, and it sends nothing read from the page unless you press the card's button. From version 0.5.66 it counts, as an anonymous daily total, that the card was shown and on which of those four sites (see "Anonymous usage statistics" below). On every other page (search engines, email, banking, anything that isn't a hotel or a booking site it reads) it shows nothing and sends nothing about the page on its own, with one exception from version 0.5.67: for 24 hours after you press a banner's button to a booking site, reaching that site's checkout or booking confirmation page is counted, as an anonymous daily total that does not say which site or page (see "Anonymous usage statistics" below). When you first install it on Chrome or Edge, it also runs once in the tabs you already have open, exactly as it would if you reloaded them, so a hotel page you were already looking at gets its comparison without a reload; it sends nothing more than a reload of that page would, and it never does this when the extension updates. (Firefox and Safari do the same by themselves.) The other automatic request on such pages is the once-a-day anonymous usage ping described below, which carries no URL, page title or page content. On a hotel page that shows no dates, it shows a small card asking for them, with two date fields and a party size (versions up to 0.5.63 also showed, if this browser checked a stay in the last 21 days whose check-in has not passed, a button naming that stay's dates and party); nothing about the page is sent unless you press one of the card's buttons, which sends the hotel's name with the dates and party size it names, like any hotel check. From version 0.5.58, if this browser checked a stay at the same hotel (the same hotel name) in the last 24 hours and its check-in has not passed, the extension checks that hotel for that stay's dates and party size by itself instead of showing the card, exactly as a press of the card's button for that stay would, and the result says so with those dates and a "Change dates" button, which shows the card instead and never checks by itself again on that page. From version 0.5.68 the same happens on a page with no dates when this browser checked a stay at any hotel in the last 21 days whose check-in has not passed: the hotel on the page is checked by itself for the newest such stay's dates and party size, the result says so with those dates and the same "Change dates" button, and if there is no such stay you see the card. The dates are not kept for later pages. If you click the Underbook toolbar button on a page, that is you asking it to check that page (in Safari on iPhone and iPad, so is choosing Underbook from the page menu): it reads the page's title or main heading as a possible hotel name, sends that name and any dates it finds to our price service like any hotel check, and counts the click as an anonymous daily total.

While Underbook is paused (from its settings page, from version 0.5.65, for 24 hours or until you turn it back on), and on a site where you turned it off, the extension does nothing on web pages: it reads nothing, checks nothing, shows nothing and sends nothing, not even its daily count. Your price watches keep running on our service until you stop them.

The price-drop mark on the icon (off unless you turn it on). Under a result with prices, on a computer, a line "Mark the toolbar if this stay's price drops" lets you turn on a mark on the Underbook icon. From version 0.5.58 that line is shown only on a browser where the mark is already on, so it can be turned off there; it is no longer offered to turn it on. It is off until you tick it, and the choice is remembered on this browser. If you never tick it, nothing in this paragraph happens: the extension never looks at a stay again by itself, and the stays you checked never leave your device on their own. Once you tick it, for that stay and for each stay you check after it, for up to 14 days after each check and while its check-in is still ahead, the extension may look at the stay's prices again by itself, while its icon is on your browser's toolbar (where the mark can be seen), at most once a day and one stay a day, when you open your first web page of the day (nothing about that page is sent). The look sends our price service what your own check of that stay sent: the hotel's name and town as the page showed them, the dates, the number of adults, the market and the currency. It is not counted as a price check by you. If the going rate is 5% or more under the one you saw when you checked, on two looks in a row, the icon shows a small blue mark; its tooltip names the stay and both figures, and a click on it opens the page where you checked that stay, which checks it again there. The mark goes away when you click it, after 3 days, at check-in, or when you check that stay again. It appears at most once for a stay and at most once a week, never while another one shows, and not on phones. Untick the line, or press "Stop these marks" on a page a mark opened, and it stops: no more looks or marks, and the details kept for it are deleted from your device. We count, as anonymous daily totals (the event name only, never the stay), that the line was shown, that it was ticked, that a look was made, that the icon was marked, that a mark was clicked, and that it was turned off.

What it reads, and what we send

  • On a hotel page, it reads the hotel name, your selected dates and the number of adults from the page, and the hotel's town when the page shows it next to the name.
  • From version 0.5.61: if a later page of the same booking on the same site, in the same tab, shows page text instead of the hotel's name (a booking step such as "Step 1 of 3", or a line such as "Book your stay"), the extension checks the hotel it checked on the earlier page of that booking, for the same dates and party, within 6 hours, instead of the page text; that name and town are kept only in that tab's storage for that site. If no hotel name can be found, it checks nothing and shows nothing.
  • From version 0.5.63: when a booking site's page gives the hotel's name in another script (for example Hebrew, Arabic, Russian, Japanese or Thai), the extension also sends the name in Latin letters that the booking site itself gives that hotel in its own page data, as a second name our price service searches only if the first finds nothing. Nothing else about the page is sent.
  • It sends exactly these to Underbook's price service (our API at underbook-backend.underbook.workers.dev) to look up and compare current rates across booking sites: the hotel name, the hotel's town as the page shows it (when it does; it tells apart hotels with the same name), your check-in and check-out dates, the number of adults (so the rates are for the right occupancy), the booking site's market and currency (so prices appear in the currency you were already looking at; the currency comes from the page, and the market from the site's country or, where the site does not say one, your browser's language setting, never from your location), and the version number of the extension. From version 0.5.71, when the page prices in a currency the service cannot check yet, it also sends a flag saying so, never which currency. Our service keeps, as daily totals with no identifier, how many checks ran in each currency and how many could not use the page's currency. For a check in a market other than the US, it also keeps, as daily totals with no identifier for each market (for example "ae"), how many answers Google Hotels priced for a visitor in another country, and how many of those were put right by asking SerpApi once more with the same details or were still priced elsewhere. We do not send your identity, your browsing history, the other pages you visit, or any payment information.
  • To find prices, our price service passes the hotel name (with its town when the page shows one), your dates, the number of adults and the market and currency to the price-data services it uses: SerpApi, which reads Google Hotels, and MakCorps, which it asks only when Google Hotels cannot be reached. Neither receives your identity, your email or anything about your device.
  • On our service, a priced result is cached for 30 minutes under the hotel, dates, party size and market, so repeat lookups are fast; that cache holds no identity. To answer a repeat check of the same hotel faster, our service also remembers for up to 24 hours which follow-up searches that hotel name needed (the name, its town if the page showed one, the market and party size, and Google's id for the property found). It holds no dates and nothing about who made the lookup. When a lookup returns no rates or fails, we keep the hotel name, its town if the page showed one, dates, market and the time it happened, with up to three of the hotel names Google Hotels gave for that lookup and how many hotels it listed, in a short rolling list (the most recent 50, kept for at most 30 days) so we can find and fix coverage gaps. That list records nothing about who made the lookup. When no hotel matched the name exactly and we picked one from a list of close matches (Google Hotels' list, or MakCorps' when MakCorps priced the stay), we keep the name you typed, the name we picked and the date in a second list with the same limits (the most recent 50, at most 30 days), so we can check that the pick was the right hotel. Our service also keeps daily totals of how long it took to answer each price check (in ranges: under 1 second, 1 to 2, 2 to 4, 4 to 8, 8 to 15, 15 to 25, or more than 25 seconds), whether the answer came from that 30-minute cache, and how many price searches it took (1, 2, 3, or 4 or more), split only by whether the check came from the extension, the web price checker or neither. These totals hold no hotel, dates, price or anything about who checked.
  • When a check's results show that the listing the banner speaks about has no free-cancellation label on Google Hotels and another booking site lists free cancellation for the same price or less, the banner says so, with both prices and a button to that site. It uses only the results of the same check and sends nothing more.
  • We don't use cookies or tracking pixels, and we don't build an advertising profile of you.

Anonymous usage statistics

To understand where people stop getting value (and fix it), the extension sends a small number of anonymous usage events to our service. Here is exactly what an event contains, and all it can ever contain:

  • No identifier (from version 0.5.65). An event carries no install id or any other identifier. The extension keeps a random install id on your device only, so that the once-a-day usage ping is counted once, and it never leaves your device. Earlier versions sent that random id with each event; our service never stored or read it.
  • Once a day, with the usage ping, whether the Underbook icon is pinned to your browser's toolbar, sent only when it is (also counted by days since install). Your browser tells the extension this; nothing else about your toolbar or your other extensions is read. Safari does not tell extensions this, so an install from the App Store never sends it.

  • From version 0.5.61, one of three event names when a hotel page showed page text instead of a hotel's name: a check used the hotel name from an earlier page of the same booking, or another name on the page, instead of the page text; or nothing was checked.   • How many days since the extension was installed, as a whole number, and on the once-a-day usage ping also as the number of calendar days (UTC) since the install date.

What these events never contain:

  • No browsing history: no URLs, no page titles, nothing about the sites you visit, apart from the words above: which of six booking sites a banner was on, or "other" (from version 0.5.58), and which of four booking sites showed the card offering to watch a booking (from version 0.5.66); and, from version 0.5.67, that the checkout or booking confirmation page of the site a banner's button opened was reached, never which site or page.

When you first install the extension (in Safari: the first time you switch it on) it opens a welcome page on underbook.co. That page counts, as daily totals with no identifier and no cookie: that it was opened by a new install, and whether you tried it, split by which way (an example hotel link or the check-a-hotel form). It also runs one live price check of its example hotel (the Bellagio, fixed dates, two adults) so you see a result at once; that check sends nothing about you and is counted as a daily total. That the welcome page was opened by a new install, that a new install reached its first hotel page, and, from the extension, its once-a-day usage ping (with the install's age in whole days, which the ping already carries), each comparison banner it showed, and the drawing and first booking-site press of a card with a lower price, are also counted, as daily totals, by the first language your browser sends with every request: English, Spanish, German, French, Portuguese (Brazil), Italian, Japanese, Korean, Dutch, Polish, Turkish, Swedish, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Chinese (Simplified), Chinese (Traditional), Portuguese (Portugal and every other country but Brazil), Hebrew, Russian, Arabic, Thai, Ukrainian, Czech, Greek, Hungarian, Romanian, Danish, Finnish, Norwegian, Slovak, Bulgarian, Croatian, Malay, or another language. These statistics exist for one purpose: to see where users drop off in the experience so we can fix it. They are not used for advertising, not shared, and not sold. Your choice: on Firefox, these statistics are sent only if you allow "technical and interaction data" for Underbook, a choice in its install prompt that you can change at any time in Firefox's Add-ons Manager (Permissions and data). On Chrome and Edge, pausing Underbook from its settings page (from version 0.5.65) stops them along with everything else, and removing the extension stops them for good. Checking hotels never depends on them.

Sharing a check from the banner (optional)

Versions before 0.5.58 show "Share this check" on a banner that shows prices (from 0.5.58 the banner no longer shows it; the web price checker still does). If you press it, the extension copies to your clipboard a short note of what that banner shows (the hotel, your dates and party size, the prices and the room-name line) and a link to the web price checker on underbook.co. The link carries only the hotel name, the check-in and check-out dates, the number of adults, the currency and a marker that it is a shared link, shared from the extension; never your email, the price you paid, the address of the page you were on, or anything else you typed. Copying sends nothing to us: the press itself is counted like the other banner events above, with no hotel, dates or link, and you decide whether to paste the note anywhere. Whoever opens the link gets the same check run again with that day's prices. Their visit is counted as described under the web price checker below, with the visits, checks and booking-site clicks from links shared from the extension counted apart, as daily totals with no identifier.

Price-drop alerts (optional, opt-in)

Only if you type your email into a price-drop alert (on the extension's banner or on the web price checker) and press its button, or tap the alert button after you have set one before, we store your email address together with the hotel (and its town, if the page showed one), dates and number of adults you asked us to watch. We use it only to email you about that stay: a confirmation, a note when a rate first appears if there was none yet, and at most one alert if the going rate is at least 5% lower on two daily checks in a row. We also keep one word for where you set the watch: which browser add-on store's copy of Underbook you used (the Chrome Web Store, Edge Add-ons or Firefox Add-ons), another copy of the extension, Underbook for Safari, or this website, so the email can name the right store. The confirmation (once the stay has a price) and the price-drop alert end with one line asking for an honest rating in that store, or, for a watch set on the website, a link to add the extension; a watch set from Underbook for Safari gets neither. These links pass through underbook.co/em, which counts that the link was opened, and in which email, and keeps nothing about you. We stop checking after your check-out date. We don't use it for marketing, we don't sell it, and we share it only with Resend, the service that sends our emails. Every email has a one-click unsubscribe link, and you can ask us at hi@underbook.co to stop a watch or delete your email at any time. Every email about a watch also ends with a link to watch another hotel from the price checker, and the price-drop alert links to the price checker with that stay filled in (the hotel name, the dates, the number of adults and the currency). These links go through underbook.co/em, where our service counts, as daily totals with no identifier, how many of them a person opened (the links back to a stay also by which kind of email they were in) and how many were opened without a person, for example by an email security scanner, and then sends you on to the price checker. Your email, the hotel and the dates are never part of those counts. We also count, as daily totals with no identifier, how many emails of each kind we sent to travelers (a confirmation, a note that rates appeared, a price alert, a rebook alert, a reminder).

Watching a stay you already booked (optional, opt-in)

If you tick "I already booked this stay" (on the web price checker, or on the extension's banner where it offers it) and press the button, a booked watch keeps only: your email, the hotel (and its town, if the page showed one), dates and number of adults, the price you paid per night and its currency, the market we price it in (for example "us" or "gb"), your free-cancellation date if you give one, and, if it is sent, the name of the site you booked on, and the one word for where you set the watch (see Price-drop alerts). We use them only to compare your price with the rates listed for the same hotel and dates once a day, and to email you about that stay: a confirmation, at most one alert if a listed rate is at least 5% lower (and at least 5 a night lower in dollars, euros or pounds, or a similar amount in your currency) on two daily checks in a row, and, for a watch set from a booking confirmation page, two reminders before your free cancellation ends: one about a week before and one the day before, sent even when no lower rate shows up (each only if the watch was set before that day began; none after a price alert or after free cancellation has ended). The price alert ends with the same one line as under Price-drop alerts (a rating in the store your copy of Underbook came from, or, for a watch set on the website, a link to add the extension), through underbook.co/em. When an alert goes out, our own inbox gets a note of the stay and the two prices, without your email, so we can check the alert was right. We stop checking after your free-cancellation date, or at check-in if you did not give one. The record is deleted automatically the day after your check-out. Every email has a one-click unsubscribe link that stops the watch and deletes the record at once. These emails also end with the link to watch another hotel, and the rebook alert and the reminders link to the price checker with that stay filled in (the hotel name, the dates, the number of adults and the currency, and for the rebook alert a marker that it is a booking), through underbook.co/em, counted as described under Price-drop alerts. Our service counts, as daily totals with no identifier, how many watches were set from a booking confirmation page, how many reminders were sent, and how many watches that asked for reminders were stopped.

A booked watch never needs your booking or confirmation number, your name, your phone number or address, or any payment details, and we do not store them. Please don't type them into the form. We don't see your booking and we don't contact the hotel or the booking site: if you rebook, you book and cancel yourself.

Watching a booking from its confirmation page (optional, opt-in)

After you book a stay on Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com or Agoda, the extension looks on that site's booking confirmation page for these details only: the hotel's name and town, your check-in and check-out dates, the number of adults, the total or nightly price and its currency, and the date your free cancellation ends. To find them it compares short pieces of text on the page with a fixed list of labels (such as "Check-in" or "Free cancellation until") and keeps only the value next to a matching label, and it reads the page's standard booking markup for the same details. It does not keep, store or send anything else from the page. It never reads your booking or confirmation number, PIN, name, email address, phone number, postal address or payment details. It looks at the site and the page's path only to recognize a confirmation page, never at the rest of the page's address, and stores or sends nothing of that address.

If it finds the hotel, the dates and a free-cancellation date at least two days away, it shows one card with those details filled in, so you can check them and change the price, the free-cancellation date, and the number of adults if the page did not show it. Nothing read from the page or about the booking leaves your device while the card is shown, and closing it or pressing "No thanks" sends nothing. From version 0.5.66, when the card is shown, the extension counts that it was shown, once for each booking, as an anonymous usage event with no identifier that names only the site (Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com or Agoda), never the hotel, the dates, the price or anything else on the page (see "Anonymous usage statistics"). Only if you press "Watch this booking" does the extension send (with the email you type, or, if you set a watch before in this browser, the email the extension kept from it, which the card uses without writing it into the page; "not you? change email" lets you type another one), as for any watch on a stay you already booked: your email, the hotel and its town, the dates, the number of adults, the price per night you confirmed (the total on the page divided by the number of nights, unless you change it), its currency, the market we price it in, the free-cancellation date and the name of the booking site, with a marker that the watch was set from a confirmation page and asks for deadline reminders.

The card is offered once per booking. To remember that, the extension keeps on your device a short code computed from the site, the hotel name and the dates, with the check-out date; it holds no booking number, name or price, and it is deleted the first day the extension runs after the check-out date.

How Underbook makes money

When you open a booking site through Underbook and complete a booking, we may earn an affiliate commission from that site, at no extra cost to you (you pay the same price you'd pay going there directly). This never changes which rates we show you or the order we show them: we always surface the genuinely lowest rate we find, and we'll link you to a cheaper option even when it earns us nothing. We never take payment from you, and we don't sell your data.

When you click through to a booking site from Underbook, the link passes through our service (/go), which adds one to a daily click total (for the web checker, split by phone or computer, whether the visit started from a shared check link and whether that link was shared from the extension, whether the check started from one of our hotel or city pages or ran in the price check box of one of our guide pages (for those, also by phone or computer), and whether the click was on one of the two booking-site searches the checker offers when it finds no price; for the web checker and the extension, whether the site you opened is one of the lesser-known booking sites we have an "Is <site> legit?" page about, never which one), noting only whether the browser's standard request headers mark it as a tap or click by a person, so crawlers following links are not counted as clicks, and forwards you to the booking site's own link. We do not record who clicked. Since November 17, 2026 one kind of link is a partner link. When a price check on our website shows a Booking.com price that comes from Booking.com's free listing on Google, and you press it, the link opens Booking.com through Stay22, an affiliate partner of Booking.com. It opens the same hotel, dates and number of adults, at the price Booking.com shows any visitor; you choose the room there. If you book, Booking.com may pay Stay22 a commission, which Stay22 shares with us, at no extra cost to you. Stay22 receives the Booking.com link (the hotel, your dates and the number of adults), a short label saying which part of our website the click came from, and what any website receives from your browser, such as your IP address. We send it nothing else. This is never done in the browser extension or the Safari app, never for visitors in the European Union, the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, never for a sponsored link, and never for another booking site: every other link stays the booking site's own, and no link is kept. One exception: when the banner has no price for your stay, its searches on other booking sites (Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com or Orbitz) open that site's own search directly, for the hotel's name, your dates and number of adults: they do not pass through our service, are not partner links and do not tell the site which page you came from, and the site receives the search as if you had typed it there.

What we never do

  • We never see, store, or transmit your card or other payment details. You always book directly on the site you choose. The only money detail we keep is the price per night you paid, and only if you set a booked watch.
  • We don't sell your data. We share it only with the services that do the work for us: the price-data services named above (the hotel, its town, dates, party size, market and currency), Resend, which sends our emails (your email and the stay it is about), Cloudflare, which runs our price service and keeps the watches, and Google Firebase, which keeps the website's anonymous page views.
  • We don't track you across the web or record your browsing.

Permissions, and why

  • Access to the sites you visit: so the comparison can appear on any hotel page, on major booking sites and individual hotels' own websites. The UI only activates when a hotel and dates are detected, or on the booking confirmation pages of Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com and Agoda as described above; nothing happens on other pages, apart from the toolbar icon's state on booking sites and the tips described above.
