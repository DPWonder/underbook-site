Is Skyscanner legit?
Yes. But it's a search engine, not the seller.

Yes, Skyscanner is legit: it launched in 2003 in Edinburgh and has been owned by Trip.com Group since 2016. So the company is real and the prices it shows are genuine quotes pulled from airlines, hotels, and booking sites. The part people miss is that Skyscanner usually doesn't sell the booking itself. It gathers prices and then hands you to another site to actually pay, and that final site sets the all-in total, the cancellation terms, and the support you get if something goes wrong. The price is not fake. It is just unfinished until you see the number at checkout. Here is how to check it.

The handoff is the whole story

Skyscanner is a metasearch engine: it collects prices from airlines, hotels, and booking sites and lines them up, but it doesn't take your payment. When you tap a result, it sends you to the seller, and the final all-in total is calculated there, where taxes, resort fees, service fees, baggage charges, and currency conversion get added. That is why the number sometimes moves between Skyscanner and checkout. It usually isn't a bait-and-switch. It is the difference between a preview and a receipt.

The second thing to know is who wins the cheap slot. On hotels, we measure this constantly: in our study (7,044 hotel prices, September 22 to October 10, 2026), some site listed the stay 5% or more below the going rate at 5,114 (73%), most often Super.com (1,340 hotels, typically 18% below), but at 4,294 of those 5,114 hotels the lowest-priced listing named no room. A cheaper listing whose room name matched turned up at just 515 of 5,830 hotels (8.8%) on our original room check (952, or 16.3%, on the stricter October 1 check) in the 14 snapshots of September 25 to October 1, 2026 that compared room names, most often on Agoda, Hotels.com and Vio.com. Matching names is strong evidence, not proof. The cheap number on a metasearch is real, but it often belongs to a seller with stricter terms and thinner support, which is exactly when you should slow down and read before you pay.

This is the same pattern we describe in why hotel prices differ across sites and same room, different price, and it applies to every metasearch, including Kayak, momondo, and trivago. The search tool is fine. The discipline is comparing final totals for the same room, on the site that actually charges your card.

Before you book a Skyscanner result, check three things

  • 1. Who actually sells it. Note the site Skyscanner sends you to, because your payment, confirmation, changes, and support all come from there.
  • 2. The all-in total. Compare the price after taxes and fees, in your own currency, not the preview shown on Skyscanner.
  • 3. The room and the terms. Confirm it's the same room type and read the cancellation policy, especially on the lowest-priced listings, which are often prepaid and non-refundable.

How to check the price yourself

Note the total the seller will charge, including every fee. Then pull the same number from the hotel's own site and one other booking site for the same room and dates. Compare totals, not headline rates. If the Skyscanner result still wins after fees, and the seller's terms are ones you can live with, it can be genuinely worth it.

Skyscanner, common questions

Is Skyscanner legit?+

Yes. Skyscanner launched in 2003 in Edinburgh and has been owned by Trip.com Group since 2016. The prices it shows are genuine quotes pulled from airlines, hotels, and booking sites. The thing to understand is that Skyscanner usually does not sell the booking itself. It sends you to another site to pay, and that site sets the final all-in total, the cancellation terms, and the customer support. Check who the actual seller is and confirm the total there before you pay.

Is Skyscanner a scam?+

No. It's a real, major metasearch and the quotes are genuine. The pattern behind most complaints is the handoff: the final total changed at the seller's checkout, or the booking was completed with a small reseller whose support was hard to reach. Neither means the price was fake. If you note who the seller is and confirm the all-in total there before paying, Skyscanner is simply a fast way to scan the market.

Why is the price different when I click through from Skyscanner?+

Because the Skyscanner number is a preview pulled from another site, and the final total is calculated when you arrive there. Taxes, resort fees, service fees, baggage fees on flights, and currency conversion get added at the seller's checkout, and inventory can shift between your search and your click. It usually is not a bait-and-switch, it is the all-in total appearing at the point of sale. Compare final totals, not headline rates.

Is it safe to book through the sites Skyscanner sends me to?+

Usually, but not equally. Skyscanner lists everyone from major booking sites and airlines to small resellers you may never have heard of, and the lowest-priced result is often one of the small ones. The booking is generally real, but support, refunds, and changes come from that seller, not from Skyscanner. Before paying a stranger for a few dollars off, check who the seller is, read their cancellation terms, and weigh whether the difference is worth the thinner support.

How do I check if a Skyscanner hotel rate is actually a good deal?+

Note the all-in total at the site Skyscanner sends you to, including taxes and fees, then compare that exact number against the hotel's own site and one other booking site for the same room and dates.
