The same stay, with and without free cancellation

At 4,518 of the 6,620 hotels, some sites listed the stay with free cancellation and others without. The lowest-priced free-cancellation listing cost a median 9% more than the lowest-priced non-refundable one; a quarter of hotels were at 0.3% or less and a quarter at 21.3% or more. At 1,756 of those hotels the extra was 5% or less, and at 1,266 hotels overall the lowest-priced listing of all already had free cancellation.

The bigger surprise is how often the site you are already on is the expensive way to get flexibility. When free cancellation is worth it

  • When it costs little or nothing extra. Check the other sites before you pay for flexibility on the one you are on.
  • When prices might drop. A cancellable booking lets you rebook if the price falls before your stay. See what to do when the price drops after you book, and how far a price has to fall before the rebook beats the non-refundable rate.
  • When you are not sure about the room. A cheaper listing often does not say which room it is. With free cancellation you can book it, read the room on the confirmation, and cancel before the deadline if it is wrong. More on same room, different price.
  • When your plans are fixed and the premium is large, a non-refundable rate can be the better choice.

Read the terms, not just the label

"Free cancellation" here is the label Google Hotels shows for a site's listed rate. It usually means free until a deadline, often a day or several days before arrival, and some rates are pay-now with a refund while others are pay-at-the-hotel. "Non-refundable" on this page means a listing without that label: most are, but some may still allow a partial refund or show their terms only on the site. Before you book, read the deadline and terms on the site itself.

Questions

How much more does free cancellation cost on a hotel?

In our 16 snapshots of 6,620 hotels (September 25 to October 10, 2026), at the 4,518 hotels that had both kinds of listing, the lowest-priced listing with free cancellation cost a median 9% more than the lowest-priced non-refundable one. At 1,756 of them (39%) the difference was 5% or less.

Can I get free cancellation for the same price on another site?

Often. At 2,168 of 6,620 hotels (33%), the lowest listed rate on at least one of Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com or Agoda had no free cancellation while another site listed free cancellation for the same price or less (within 2%).

Is free cancellation worth it?

When it costs little or nothing extra, usually yes: you keep the option to rebook if the price drops, to change plans, or to cancel if the room on the confirmation is not the one you wanted. When the premium is large and your dates are fixed, a non-refundable rate can be the better choice. Always read the cancellation deadline.
