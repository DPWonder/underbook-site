The verdict, in plain terms

Price match guarantees are real, but they're built to be hard to claim and are shrinking. They're a fine bonus to invoke if you happen to spot a cheaper identical rate, but a poor thing to rely on instead of checking the price up front.

What the guarantee gives you

  • ✅ A real path to a refund if you find an identical cheaper rate in time
  • ✅ Booking.com still runs "We Price Match," claimable before check-in
  • ✅ Some hotel chains match a lower rate, but only if you booked direct
  • ✅ Occasionally worth a few minutes to claim when the gap is large

Why it usually doesn't lower what you pay

  • ⚠️ The burden is on you to find, screenshot and prove the cheaper rate
  • ⚠️ Every condition must match: room, dates, occupancy, cancellation policy
  • ⚠️ Member-only, package and currency-shifted rates are routinely rejected
  • ⚠️ Expedia and Hotels.com dropped their guarantee entirely on July 28, 2026

How a price match claim really works

The mechanics are almost always the same. You book first. Then, if you later find the same stay for less somewhere else, you contact the booking site (usually with a screenshot) and ask them to match it. They verify the rate and, if it qualifies, either adjust your reservation or refund the difference. On Booking.com's "We Price Match," the lower price has to be for the same room type, dates, cancellation policy and occupancy, and you have to claim it before check-in. That's a genuine promise. It's also a narrow one, and the narrowness is the point.

Here's why so many claims quietly fail. The "cheaper" rate you found is a member-only or app-only price, which doesn't count as publicly available. Or it's a package rate bundled with a flight, so it isn't the same product. Or the cancellation policy is different: a non-refundable rate can't be matched against your refundable one. Or the currency is different and the "lower" number vanishes once it's converted. Or the rate simply sold out before an agent could verify it. Each of these is a legitimate reason to deny a claim, and together they're why a guarantee that sounds airtight ends up honored a small fraction of the time. The rules differ by where you booked. Booked direct with Marriott or Hilton? Their specific rules are in hotel best rate guarantees after booking, and Booking.com's are in Booking.com price dropped after you booked.

Before you rely on any guarantee, check three things

  • 1. Who holds the reservation. A hotel chain's best-rate guarantee almost always requires you to have booked direct with the chain; a third-party booking means only that site's policy applies.
  • 2. Whether it still exists. Expedia and Hotels.com ended their Hotel Price Guarantee on July 28, 2026. Don't assume a program is still running because it used to be.
  • 3. Whether the rates truly match. Room type, dates, occupancy and cancellation policy all have to line up, all-in after taxes and fees; a difference in any one of them sinks the claim.

The reliable version: compare before you book

A price match guarantee is protection after the fact: it asks you to catch a cheaper identical rate once you've already committed, then win a claim against fine print. Flip the order and the whole problem disappears: if you compare the same room across sites before you book, you just book the cheaper one, and there's nothing to claim. The only reason people don't do that is that it's tedious: opening five tabs, matching the exact room and dates, and adding up taxes and fees on each one is exactly the work the guarantees are quietly counting on you to skip.

Price match guarantees: common questions

Are hotel price match guarantees a scam?+

No. They're real promises, and sites like Booking.com do honor qualifying claims. But they're written to be narrow, and they put all the work on you: you have to find the exact same room, dates, occupancy and cancellation policy cheaper somewhere else, prove it, and claim it in a short window before check-in. Most claims fail because the cheaper rate turns out to be member-only, a package, a different cancellation policy, or a different currency. It's not fraud, it's fine print, so treat the guarantee as a possible bonus, not a reason to skip checking the price up front.

Did Expedia and Hotels.com really end their price guarantee?+

Yes. The two sites share the One Key program and discontinued their Hotel Price Guarantee on July 28, 2026, with no replacement announced. Bookings made before that date earned at the old rates, but the after-the-fact price protection is gone. If you booked with either site expecting to be able to claim a lower price later, that safety net no longer exists, which is a good reason to compare the price before you book rather than after.

How do I actually win a Booking.com price match?+

Find the same room type, same dates, same occupancy and same cancellation policy at a lower publicly available price, capture a clear screenshot showing all of that, and contact customer service before check-in. If it verifies, they'll adjust your reservation or refund the difference. The claims that succeed are the ones where every condition matches exactly and the cheaper rate is still live when an agent checks, so move fast, and make sure you're comparing a truly identical rate, not a member-only or package rate that looks lower but doesn't qualify.

Can I use a hotel brand's best-rate guarantee if I booked through Expedia or Booking.com?+

Almost never. Hotel chain best-rate guarantees are designed to reward booking direct, so they generally require that the reservation was made on the chain's own site or app. If you booked the same hotel through a third-party site, that site owns the reservation and the chain's guarantee doesn't apply; your only price-adjustment path is that site's own policy. Always check who holds your booking before you count on a chain guarantee.
