How often a 5% drop alert would have written

Our earlier price watcher read the going rate for one stay, July 25 to 28, 2026, at 12 US hotels once a day from June 3. Run with the checker's rule (5% below the first read on two daily reads in a row):

  • The alert fired at 7 of the 12 hotels; with a 10% line, at 6.
  • At 8 of the 12 the last read was more than 2% above June 3, and 3 of them had fired an alert on the way: an alert is a moment to act, not a trend.
  • Across 608 hotels priced twice a week apart for the same stay, the going rate fell 5% or more at 160 of 608 (26%) and rose 5% or more at 236 of 608 (39%).

So any of these alerts will catch real drops. What turns a drop into a lower price is a refundable booking you can cancel after you rebook.

What Underbook does differently

Underbook is built on the same list: for your hotel and dates it reads the booking sites Google Hotels lists (a median of 12 per hotel in our 16 price snapshots of September 25 to October 10, 2026). It does not search anywhere else. What it adds are three checks, with what they found in those snapshots (6,620 hotel prices):

Whether the lower price names the same room. At 4,783 of 6,620 (72%) hotel prices a listing was 5% or more below the going rate, typically 17% below. That listing named a room at 775 of 4,783 (16%), and named the same room as a listing at the going rate at 571 of 4,783 (12%). Underbook says which: "same room name", or that the lower listing needs a check before you book.

Taxes and fees as each site reports them. It shows each price all-in where the sites report taxes and fees (a resort or destination fee may be extra). That matters because sellers report differently: where taxes added 10% or more, KAYAK's listed price sat near the before-tax rate at 37% of hotels, Booking.com's at 8%.

The hotel's own site beside Booking.com. Google Hotels listed the hotel's own site at 4,425 of 6,620 (67%) hotel prices. Like with like against Booking.com, the hotel's site was more than 2% lower at 26% of 3,345 hotels and Booking.com at 13%; within 2% at 61%.

One email for the stay, across sites. Its price-drop email follows the going rate, the price most booking sites list for your stay, so one site's promotion does not trigger it alone; when it writes, the checker shows each site's price and whether a lower listing names the same room.

How to make a price alert pay off

  1. Book a refundable rate and note its free-cancellation deadline.
  2. Set one alert for that hotel and those dates (Google Hotels, KAYAK, trivago or Underbook).
  3. When it writes, open the lower price and match the room, the cancellation terms and the total.
  4. Book the lower rate first, then cancel the first booking before its deadline.
