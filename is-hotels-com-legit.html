Is Hotels.com legit?
Short answer: yes, with real tradeoffs.

Yes. Hotels.com is a real, established booking platform owned by Expedia Group, the same parent as Expedia and Vrbo, and confirmed bookings go through. It is sometimes cheaper than booking direct, but in our data it more often matched the hotel's own price or came in above it. It has booked hotels since 1991. The catches are that the lowest rates are frequently prepaid and non-refundable, resort fees can still be added at the property, the beloved collect-10-nights reward became the One Key program in 2023, and support runs through Hotels.com rather than the hotel. Here is the honest breakdown, and how to check whether a given price actually holds up.

The verdict, in plain terms

Legit, and sometimes cheaper than the hotel's own site, though usually not; a cheap rate can be prepaid, and the loyalty program that made it worth staying loyal isn't what it used to be.

Why is it sometimes cheaper than the hotel's own site?

This is the question that makes people suspicious, and the answer is mostly boring, not shady. Hotels.com negotiates room blocks in bulk and can discount them, and it offers member-only prices when you're signed in. Hotels also release quieter, lower rates through these channels to fill rooms without publicly cutting their own headline price.

How often that happens, from our September 25 snapshot (stays 35 days out, US market, USD): at the 225 hotels where Google Hotels listed both Hotels.com and the hotel's own site, Hotels.com was more than 1% cheaper at 44, within 1% at 104, and more than 1% higher at 77. So check both. When it is cheaper, what you often trade for the lower price is flexibility: the lowest rates are prepaid and non-refundable, and resort fees and local taxes can still be added at the property or at checkout, so the headline can look lower than the all-in total. That is a fine trade when your dates are locked, and a worse one when your plans might move.

Before you book a prepaid Hotels.com rate, check three things

  • 1. The all-in total. Compare the price after taxes and fees, including any resort fee, not the headline nightly rate.
  • 2. The rate type. A prepaid rate can be cheaper, but confirm whether it's refundable before you commit.
  • 3. The cancellation terms. A $40 difference isn't worth it if your plans might change and the rate is non-refundable.

The reward-program change most people miss

For years, the reason to stay loyal to Hotels.com was dead simple: collect 10 nights, get 1 reward night worth the average of what you paid. It was easy to understand and it added up. In 2023, Expedia Group retired that stamp-card program and merged Hotels.com into its One Key rewards program, which earns OneKeyCash you can spend across Expedia, Hotels.com, and Vrbo.

One Key isn't a scam, but it works differently. A lot of longtime users feel the new program is less generous and harder to reason about than banking whole free nights. The practical takeaway: if part of why you're booking on Hotels.com is the rewards, check what you actually earn on this specific booking before you count it as a discount, rather than assuming it works the way it used to.

Hotels.com: common questions

Is Hotels.com a scam?+

No. It's a real booking platform owned by Expedia Group, and the hotel bookings are genuine. The frustration people report is almost always about the terms, non-refundable prepaid rates, resort fees added at the property, the loyalty program changing in 2023, and support running through Hotels.com rather than the hotel, rather than the booking not existing. Read the rate type and confirm the all-in total before you commit and you avoid the most common complaints.

Is it safe to pay on Hotels.com?+

Yes. Payments go through a major, established company, and confirmed bookings are real. The thing to watch isn't payment safety, it's the rate's terms: whether it's prepaid or refundable, and what the all-in total is once taxes and any resort fee are added. Check those before you pay and the transaction itself is as routine as any other large booking site.

Do the Hotels.com rewards still give free nights?+

Not in the old way. The collect-10-nights-get-1-free stamp card was retired in 2023 and replaced by One Key, which earns OneKeyCash you can spend across Expedia, Hotels.com, and Vrbo rather than banking whole reward nights. It still has value, but many longtime users find it less generous and harder to reason about. If rewards are part of why you're booking, check what this specific stay actually earns before counting it as a discount.

Can I cancel a Hotels.com booking?+

It depends on the rate. Many hotels offer a refundable option cancellable within a window, while the lowest prepaid rates are non-refundable with no changes. Cancellations and problems go through Hotels.com rather than the hotel, which can be slower than dealing with the property directly. Always check the specific rate's terms before paying, this is the single most important thing to verify on any prepaid rate.
