What is the cheapest site to book hotels?
Honest answer: there isn't one. Here's the data.

So how do you actually pay less?

Don't expect the household names to settle it. Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Priceline and their siblings share supply and sign rate parity agreements, yet at the 245 hotels listing both Expedia and Booking.com in our September 25 snapshot, their lowest prices were within $2 a night at only 54%. Even Booking.com, listed at 90% of hotels, was at or below every other site at only 846 of 4,729 hotels (18%) where its price could be compared like with like (does Booking.com have the cheapest prices?). The going rate is the price most sites agree on, and the lowest-priced listing often comes from another class of site: resellers (Super.com, Vio.com, Algotels and similar) below it, with strings attached: prepaid, strict cancellation, and room details you must verify yourself.

That gives you an honest decision rule. If your dates are locked and you'll verify the room and terms before paying, a wholesale rate is where the real price gaps are; treat it as a lead worth checking, not a sure thing. If you value flexibility or loyalty benefits, book refundable at the going rate, direct with the hotel, and know the trade: the cheaper listing was typically 17% below, but at 4,294 of the 5,114 hotels with one it named no room. The extreme end of the discount-for-certainty trade is opaque booking, where even the hotel's name stays hidden until you pay; we've broken down when Priceline Express Deals are worth it separately.

Do those lower prices hold on the site itself? In a check on November 3, 2026, 19 of 20 lower prices Underbook showed were the same price on the booking site about 5 hours later (within 5%), though some were for a different room or did not state cancellation terms (do lower hotel prices hold?).

And use time as well as tabs: hotel prices move constantly (in our early tracking, half the hotels we watched changed price within a single day). A refundable booking you keep watching can still catch a later dip. That's what price drop alerts are for.

The 3-step cheap-booking routine

  • 1. Get the going-rate baseline. Check two mainstream sites or the hotel's own site for your room's all-in price. The lower one is the number to beat.
  • 2. Check the wholesale layer. If a reseller quotes meaningfully less, verify the room type, all-in total, and cancellation terms before paying.
  • 3. Let time work. If your trip is weeks out, watch the price and book on a drop instead of accepting today's number.

Where to book: questions, answered honestly

Is there really no single cheapest booking site?+

Really. Even the big brands split: at the 245 hotels listing both Expedia and Booking.com in our September 25 snapshot, Expedia was more than 2% cheaper at 49 and Booking.com at 56. The site with the lowest price varies hotel by hotel. In our study of September 22 to October 10, 2026, Super.com was lowest most often, yet only at 1,340 of the 5,114 hotels where some site beat the going rate by 5% or more. Any article naming one permanent winner is selling something.

Do the big sites all show the same price?+

Often not. Hotels distribute rates to major channels, and parity agreements plus competitive matching pull the public price together, but not all the way: at the 245 hotels listing both Expedia and Booking.com in our September 25 snapshot, their lowest prices were within $2 a night at 54%. That's why we compare them all, and why we wrote a whole page on whether the same room is cheaper elsewhere.

The wholesale rate is 30% cheaper. What's the catch?+

Three usual suspects: it's prepaid and non-refundable, the room may be a different class than the one you compared (resellers rarely publish full room detail), and taxes or fees may only surface at final checkout. In our snapshots of September 22 to October 10, 2026, Super.com named the room at none of the 1,340 hotels where it was lowest and showed no free-cancellation label at any of the 3,740 hotels listing it. Verify those three things before trusting that discount; skip that and you're gambling.

Do hotel comparison sites show every booking site?+

No. Metasearch engines show sites they have relationships with, and the ordering reflects commercial factors, not just price. Wholesale rates are frequently buried pages deep or missing entirely. Underbook shows the lowest price among the booking sites Google Hotels lists for your hotel and dates, smaller sites included, and says whether that listing names the same room; a site Google Hotels does not list is missing from its comparison too.

How does Underbook make money?+

So far it hasn't; it's a free tool built to prove that honest comparison is useful. If you book through Underbook we may earn a commission, which never changes which rate we show. No booking site pays us for placement, which is exactly why we can show a wholesaler above a household name when the wholesaler is cheaper, and say to check before booking when the room names don't match.
