Start with the two that almost always work

If you only do two things, do these. They don't depend on luck, timing, or a coupon code.

Tactic 1

Compare the all-in total, not the headline

The nightly number you sort by often leaves out taxes and mandatory fees. A room at $180 plus a $45 resort fee costs more than a $210 all-in room next to it. Compare the price after taxes and fees for your exact dates, across the hotel site and one or two booking sites. This is the single highest-yield habit.

Tactic 2

Book refundable, then rebook if it drops

Lock in a fully-refundable rate now so you can't lose your spot, then keep an eye on the price. Hotel rates move with demand, so the same room often gets cheaper before your stay. If it drops, rebook at the lower rate and cancel the old one. You get the downside protection without gambling on when to book. Five more that work in the right situation

These help when they apply. The trick is knowing when to reach for each one instead of treating them as a sure discount.

3. Check the direct rate before you book a third party

Hotels sometimes match or beat booking-site prices on their own site, and direct rates can carry perks: free breakfast, a better cancellation policy, loyalty points. Not always cheaper, but a two-minute check that often pays off. See exactly when Booking.com beats direct and when it doesn't, then compare the same room and dates on both before deciding.

4. Look at wholesalers, but match the room, not the discount

Sites like Super.com and Vio.com show genuinely low wholesale rates, but a headline "60% off" is often a non-refundable or opaque room compared against a flexible one. When you match the exact room type and cancellation terms, the gap usually shrinks. Worth it when your dates are locked. See our honest reads on whether Super.com and Vio.com are legit.

5. Use a loyalty or membership rate you already have

Free hotel loyalty programs, AAA/AARP, and some credit-card travel portals unlock a lower member rate or perks on the same room. It's free to sign up and takes one click at booking. Worth it when you're already a member; not worth chasing a new signup for a single night. And check whether a booking site's own loyalty rate actually wins: whether Booking.com Genius is worth it comes down to the all-in total, not the badge.

6. Be flexible on dates or neighborhood when you can

Shifting a stay by a night, or picking a hotel a few blocks off the main strip, can cut the rate noticeably because price tracks demand. Only useful when your trip is flexible, but when it is, it's one of the bigger levers.

7. Skip the tricks that mostly don't work

Incognito mode doesn't reliably lower hotel prices; the "prices go up if you look twice" idea is largely a myth for hotels. Neither does obsessively hunting for a promo code on a random coupon site. Comparing the all-in total (Tactic 1) beats both, without the folklore.

Cheaper hotel rates: common questions

How do I find the lowest price for a hotel online?+

Compare the price after taxes and fees for the same room and dates on the hotel's own site and on each booking site, side by side, and book the lowest confirmed one. Google Hotels lists many sites, but its first number often leaves out taxes and fees; the free check on underbook.co and the free Underbook extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari show each site's total for your own hotel and dates, and whether a lower price names the same room.

What's the single most reliable way to pay less?+

Compare the all-in total (the price after taxes and mandatory fees for your exact room and dates) across the hotel's own site and one or two booking sites, and book the lowest confirmed one. Most of the money people overpay comes from comparing the headline nightly rate instead of the real total. It works consistently because it doesn't rely on timing or luck.

Is there a best day of the week to book a hotel?+

Not really. Hotel prices track demand, not a fixed weekly cycle, so there's no dependable "book on Tuesday" rule. Last-minute discounts happen when a hotel has unsold rooms, but prices can also rise as a busy date fills up. Booking a refundable rate now and rebooking if it drops is a more reliable way to get the low price without guessing the timing.

Are the huge wholesaler discounts real?+

The companies are legit and the rates can be genuinely lower, but a 30 to 60 percent "discount" often compares a non-refundable or opaque room against a flexible standard one. Match the exact room type and cancellation terms and the gap usually narrows. Still worth it when your dates are certain; just compare like-for-like.

Does browsing in incognito get me a lower rate?+

For hotels, mostly no. The idea that prices jump because a site "sees you looking twice" is largely a myth here, and incognito isn't a reliable lever. Your time is better spent comparing the all-in total across a couple of sites, which actually surfaces a cheaper price when one exists.
