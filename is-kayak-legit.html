Is Kayak legit?
Yes, but it's a search tool, not the store.

Yes, Kayak is legit: a real, established metasearch engine owned by Booking Holdings, the same parent as Booking.com, Priceline, and Agoda. The prices it shows are genuine quotes pulled from other sites. The part people miss is that Kayak usually doesn't sell the room itself. It gathers prices and then sends you to a booking site or the hotel to actually pay, and that final site is where taxes, fees, and currency can move the number. Here is the honest breakdown, and how to check whether a given price actually holds up.

Why is the price different when I go to book?

This is the complaint that makes people wonder if Kayak is trustworthy, and the answer is mostly boring, not shady. Kayak is a metasearch engine: it collects prices from booking sites and hotels and shows them side by side, but it doesn't take your payment. When you click a result, it sends you to that site, and the final all-in total is calculated there, where taxes, resort fees, service fees, and currency conversion get added. Inventory can also shift between your search and your click, and some results are paid placements.

So the Kayak number is real, it's just a preview rather than a promise. That's a fine way to find candidates fast, and a poor way to know the exact price you'll pay. The number that matters is the all-in total for the same room, on the site that actually books it, and Kayak by design doesn't show you that until you leave. The same preview-versus-checkout gap applies to every metasearch, including Skyscanner and Kayak's own sibling momondo. In 14 snapshots of our hotel price study (September 25 to October 1, 2026), KAYAK.com and momondo.com listed the same price before tax, to the dollar, at all but one of 938 hotels where both appeared on Google Hotels, and KAYAK.com and Hotelscombined.com, a third Booking Holdings search site, matched at all 1,045. That is the signed-out listed price, not a room-by-room check. So checking momondo after Kayak adds little.

Before you trust a Kayak price, check three things

  • 1. Who actually books it. Note which site Kayak sends you to, since your payment, confirmation, and support come from there.
  • 2. The all-in total. Compare the price after taxes and fees, in your own currency, not the preview shown on Kayak.
  • 3. The room and terms. Confirm it's the same room type and read the cancellation policy before you pay.

Kayak: common questions

Is Kayak a scam?+

No. It's a real, established metasearch engine owned by Booking Holdings, and the prices it shows are genuine quotes from other sites. The frustration people report is almost always about the handoff, the final total changing at the site Kayak sends you to, or booking with an OTA you didn't expect, rather than the price not existing. Note who actually books the room and confirm the all-in total there, and you avoid the most common complaints.

Do I book directly on Kayak?+

Usually not. For most hotel results Kayak passes you to a booking site or the hotel to complete the reservation, so your payment, confirmation, cancellation, and support come from that final site. Kayak does have its own booking flow in some cases, but the default experience is metasearch. Always check who the merchant is before entering your card.

Why did the price go up after I clicked?+

The Kayak result is a preview pulled from another site, and the final total is calculated when you arrive there. Taxes, resort fees, service fees, and currency conversion can be added at checkout, and inventory can change between the search and the click. It usually isn't a bait-and-switch, it's the all-in total appearing at the point of sale. Check the full breakdown before you approve the charge.

No. We have no affiliate relationship with Kayak. This page isn't a referral pitch, it's an honest answer to a question a lot of travelers ask. Underbook surfaces whichever site has the cheaper rate, says whether it names your room, and shows the lower price even when it earns nothing from it.
