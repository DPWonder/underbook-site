First, check what kind of rate you booked

This is the only thing that decides your options. Find your confirmation email and look for the cancellation policy.

Refundable / free cancellation

If your booking can be cancelled for free until a certain date, you are in the strongest position. You can rebook the lower rate and cancel the old one, as long as you act before the cancellation deadline. Jump to the rebook and cancel steps, or see what free cancellation usually costs.

Non-refundable / prepaid

If your rate is non-refundable, you cannot cancel for a refund, so rebooking usually will not help. Your play is a price-match claim or a polite ask to the hotel. Jump to the price-match steps.

If your rate is refundable: rebook, then cancel

This is the cleanest way to capture a drop, and it works whether the lower price is on the same site or a different one. Do it in this order so you are never left without a room. The longer checklist, including the time zone of your cancellation deadline, is in how to rebook a hotel for a lower price.

  1. 1

    Confirm the new rate is the same room. Same hotel, same dates, same room type, and check whether taxes and resort fees are included. A cheaper headline that drops the fees later is not really cheaper. Our hotel price comparison and resort fees guide cover the traps.

  2. 2

    Book the new, lower rate first. Lock it in while it is live. Rates move, so securing the cheaper reservation before you cancel the old one means you are never stuck without a booking.

  3. 3

    Cancel the old reservation before its deadline. Double-check the new booking is confirmed, then cancel the original and keep the cancellation confirmation. This is the step people miss, because the deadline is easy to forget after you have moved on.

The whole game is timing. A drop only helps if you find out while the old rate is still cancellable. That is exactly what a price drop alert is for. Already booked? If your rate is non-refundable: ask for a price match

You cannot rebook your way out of a prepaid rate, but a lower price still gives you something to work with. Many major hotel chains publish a price-match policy, and some booking sites do too. Terms vary a lot, and you usually have to file the claim quickly with a screenshot of the cheaper rate, so read the policy on your confirmation before you assume anything.

If there is no formal policy, it is still worth a polite message to the hotel directly. Front-desk and reservations teams can sometimes match a lower public rate or add value, especially if you booked directly. It may not work, but the ask costs nothing. Price dropped after booking: common questions

Can I get a refund if my hotel price drops after I book?+

It depends on your rate. On a fully refundable booking you can rebook the lower price and cancel the old reservation before its deadline, so you effectively capture the drop. On a non-refundable rate there is no automatic refund, but some hotels and booking sites offer a price-match policy you can claim under. Check the policy on your confirmation.

Do hotels refund the difference automatically?+

No. Many major chains have a price-match policy that can refund the difference or give a credit, but you have to find the cheaper same room and file a claim, usually fast. For an ordinary refundable booking, rebooking at the lower rate and cancelling the old one is simpler and more reliable.

What if my rate is non-refundable?+

You cannot cancel a non-refundable rate for a refund, so rebooking usually will not help. Try the hotel or booking site's price-match policy if it has one, or contact the hotel directly and politely ask them to match a lower public rate. Neither is certain, but both are free to try.
