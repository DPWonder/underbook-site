The catch with cheap hotel rates

A lower price only counts if it's the same room on the same terms. Booking sites are full of near-matches: a "city view" swapped for a "standard," a fully-refundable rate quietly replaced with a non-refundable one, a resort fee that only appears at checkout. A comparison that ignores those differences isn't lowering your price; it's changing your booking.

Underbook's whole job is to make the comparison honest. It compares the room names each site publishes and tells you whether the cheaper listing names the same room. When it can't confirm the two rooms are identical, it says so plainly and shows you both. It never pretends a lookalike is the same room to win a click.

Which hotel comparison site actually gets you the lowest price?

We measured this rather than guessed. Recording 7,044 hotel prices in 368 cities on every booking site Google Hotels lists (17 snapshots, September 22 to October 10, 2026), two patterns held. First, a lower listing is common: at 73% of hotels some site was 5% or more below the going rate, typically 16.7% below, and the big names hold rate parity less tightly than advertised: on the same tax footing, Expedia and Booking.com were within $2 a night at 59% of the 3,056 hotels where both could be compared, in our 14 price snapshots of September 25 to October 1, 2026. Second, the lower listing rarely proves it is your room: at 4,294 of 5,114 hotels with one, the lowest listing named no room, and in our 14 price snapshots of September 25 to October 1, 2026, a lower listing whose room name matched turned up at 515 of 5,830 hotels (8.8%) on our original room check (952, or 16.3%, on the stricter October 1 check), strong evidence but not proof.

How to compare hotel prices the right way

A genuine hotel room comparison lines up four things, not just the headline nightly rate:

  • 1. The same room. A "city view king" is not a "standard queen." Confirm the room name matches before you compare prices.
  • 2. The all-in total. Compare the price with taxes, resort fees, and cleaning fees included. A lower base rate with a hidden resort fee often costs more at checkout.
  • 3. The cancellation terms. A cheaper non-refundable rate is a different product than a refundable one. Compare like for like.
  • 4. The same dates and occupancy. Prices swing by date and guest count, so hold those constant across every site.

Doing all four by hand for every booking is tedious enough that almost nobody does it. That is exactly the job Underbook automates: it compares hotel prices for your hotel and dates across sites on an all-in basis and flags whether the lowest-priced listing names the same room or is an unverified lead to check.

Hotel price comparison: common questions

How do I compare hotel prices across booking sites?+

Look up one hotel and your dates on Google Hotels, which lists the booking sites side by side, then compare the totals with taxes and fees for the same room name and the same cancellation terms, and check the hotel's own site too. A lower listing is common and often names no room: in our 17 price snapshots of September 22 to October 10, 2026, some booking site listed the stay 5% or more below the going rate at 5,114 of 7,044 hotel prices (73%), and at 4,294 of those the lowest listing named no room. Underbook's free browser extension and web price checker show each booking site's Google Hotels price for your hotel and dates and say whether a lower listing names the same room.

What is the best hotel comparison website?+

No single booking site had the lowest price everywhere: in our 14 price snapshots of September 25 to October 1, 2026, Booking.com's public price was at or below every other site's at only 846 of 4,729 hotels (18%), compared like with like. Look for one that shows the price with taxes and fees where the sites report them, includes the smaller sites as well as the big ones, and says whether a lower listing names the same room. Underbook is a free browser extension that does this for the booking sites Google Hotels lists for your hotel and dates. It sees only the public prices those sites list there, not member prices or sites Google Hotels leaves out, and a matching room name is not proof of the same room.

Are hotel comparison sites accurate?+

The nightly rate they show is usually accurate, but it can be misleading. Many hotel comparison sites rank listings by commercial factors, quote a base rate before taxes and resort fees, and compare a cheaper room against the one you actually want. When we recorded 7,044 hotel prices from September 22 to October 10, 2026, the lowest listing named no room at 4,294 of the 5,114 hotels where some site was 5% or more below the going rate. So treat the headline number as a starting point, then confirm the room, all-in total, and cancellation terms before you book.

How do I compare hotel prices for the same room?+

The trap is comparing a cheaper rate that's really a different room or a non-refundable version. Underbook shows the price each booking site lists on Google Hotels for your hotel and dates, says whether a lower listing names the same room and whether it shows free cancellation, and says to check before booking when the room names don't match. A matching room name is strong evidence, not proof, so read the room and the cancellation terms on the site before you book.

Why is the same room cheaper on a site I've never heard of?+

Lesser-known booking sites buy rooms through different wholesale channels and spend less on advertising, so their listed rates are sometimes lower. Not always the identical room, though, which is exactly why Underbook tells you whether the cheaper listing names the same room, so you can check before you book.

No. Underbook is a free browser extension. If you book through a site it surfaces, it may earn a small commission from that site; you pay nothing extra, and it shows the lower rate even when its commission would be smaller.
