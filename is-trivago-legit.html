Honest review

Is trivago legit?
Short answer: yes, with one catch.

Yes. Trivago is a real, established hotel metasearch, founded in 2005 in Germany, publicly traded on the Nasdaq, and majority-owned by Expedia Group. It doesn't sell rooms itself. It compares prices from booking sites and the hotel, then sends you off to one of them to book. The one catch: booking sites bid for the price it puts at the top, so that highlighted price is partly a paid placement, and the site it hands you to sets the real terms and the final all-in total. So the highlighted number isn't always the lowest once taxes and fees land. Here's the honest breakdown, and how to check whether a given price actually holds up.

How a metasearch actually makes money

This is the part that trips people up, and the answer is mostly boring, not shady. Trivago is a metasearch, not a booking site. It gathers a headline price for a hotel from lots of booking sites, lines them up, and earns a referral fee when you click through to one of them. Because that fee is how it gets paid, the most prominent slot on the page, the big highlighted price, is one booking sites can bid to occupy. It's a real price, but it's partly an ad, not purely the lowest total available.

That has two practical consequences. First, the headline rate trivago shows can exclude taxes, resort fees, or currency conversion that only appear at the booking site's checkout, so the number you compare isn't always the number you pay. Second, trivago hands you off to another site to actually book, which means that site sets the cancellation terms, the support, and the final all-in charge, not trivago. None of that makes it a scam. It just means the highlighted price is a starting point to verify, not a finish line. The same referral model runs every metasearch, from Kayak to Skyscanner.

Before you trust the highlighted trivago price, check three things

  • 1. The all-in total. Compare the price after taxes and fees, and in your own currency, not the headline nightly rate.
  • 2. The hotel's own site. Check it directly too. A member or loyalty rate can beat every OTA and sometimes isn't in trivago's list.
  • 3. The cancellation terms. The lowest price shown is often prepaid and non-refundable, which isn't worth a few dollars if your plans might change.

One thing on trivago's side is not the metasearch: trivago DEALS, a separate booking site at trivago.deals. Its own site says a booking there is a booking directly with a licensed trivago brand, so there you do book and pay with trivago DEALS. Trivago: common questions

Is trivago a scam?+

No. It's a real, publicly traded hotel metasearch majority-owned by Expedia Group, and the prices it shows come from genuine booking sites. The frustration people report is almost always about the highlighted price being a paid slot and the final total moving once taxes and fees are added at the booking site, rather than the booking not existing. Verify the all-in total and check the hotel's own site before you commit and you avoid the most common complaints.

Why is the price higher once I click through to book?+

This is the most common surprise on trivago. The headline rate it displays can exclude taxes, resort fees, and currency conversion that only appear at the booking site's checkout, and the booking site, not trivago, sets the final all-in charge. It usually isn't a bait price, it's an incomplete headline that fills in at checkout. Always check the full breakdown on the booking site so the price you approve is the price you pay.

Do I book on trivago or somewhere else?+

Somewhere else. Trivago doesn't process the booking itself, it sends you to a booking site or the hotel to complete it. That means the cancellation policy, the customer support, and the payment all sit with the site you get sent to, not with trivago. Always read that site's specific rate terms at checkout, this is the single most important thing to verify before paying.

Is trivago DEALS legit?+

It is a real booking site under the trivago name, and a different thing from the trivago comparison. Its own site, trivago.deals, says that when you book there you are booking directly with a licensed trivago brand, so you pay trivago DEALS and its terms apply. Google Hotels lists it as a seller like any booking site; the measured box above shows how often it had the lowest price, whether it named the room, and how often it showed free cancellation. Algotels now runs under the same name (its footer reads Algotels by trivago DEALS Ltd.).
