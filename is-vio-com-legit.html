Why is it so much cheaper than the hotel's own site?

This is the question that makes people suspicious, and the answer is boring, not shady. Vio.com sources rooms through wholesale distribution channels, the same kind of back-end supply that powers a lot of "member rate" and discount-booking sites. Hotels release inventory to those channels below their public rate to keep rooms full, and a reseller can pass part of that discount on to win your booking. It also spends far less on brand advertising than the household-name travel sites, so it doesn't need a fat margin on every room.

The lower prices are real. What you're trading for them is flexibility and simplicity: the lowest rates are prepaid and non-refundable, the final charge can include taxes, fees, or currency conversion that weren't in the headline, and if a booking goes sideways you're dealing with Vio's support rather than walking up to the hotel desk. That's a fine trade when your dates are locked, and a worse one when they aren't.

Before you book a prepaid wholesale rate, check three things

  • 1. The all-in total. Compare the price after taxes and fees, in your own currency, not the headline nightly rate.
  • 2. The room type. Make sure it's the same room you'd book direct, not a smaller or "run of house" version.
  • 3. The cancellation terms. A $40 difference isn't worth it if your plans might change and the rate is non-refundable.

Vio.com: common questions

Is Vio.com a scam?+

No. It's a real, operating booking platform and the hotel bookings are genuine. The frustration people report is almost always about the terms: prepaid, non-refundable rates, slower cancellations, and occasionally a final charge that differs from the headline once taxes or currency conversion are added, rather than the booking not existing. Confirm the all-in total and the cancellation policy before you commit and you avoid the most common complaints.

Why is my card charged a different amount than the price shown?+

This is the most common surprise with discount booking sites, Vio included. The headline nightly rate may not include taxes and local fees, and if the room is priced in another currency your bank's conversion (plus any foreign-transaction fee) can move the final number. It usually isn't a hidden overcharge; it's the all-in total in your currency. Always check the full breakdown at checkout so the price you approve is the price you pay.

Can I cancel a Vio.com booking?+

It depends on the rate you booked. Some are refundable within a window; the lowest-priced ones are non-refundable. Cancellation and changes go through Vio.com rather than the hotel, which is slower than dealing with the property directly. Always check the specific rate's terms at checkout: this is the single most important thing to verify before paying.
